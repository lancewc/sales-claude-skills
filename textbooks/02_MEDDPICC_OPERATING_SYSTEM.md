## CHAPTER 1: THE ORIGIN & PHILOSOPHY OF MEDDPICC

In the 1990s, **Parametric Technology Corporation (PTC)** grew from a startup into an enterprise software behemoth, posting **40 consecutive quarters of record revenue growth**. 

The engine behind this legendary execution was engineered by **John McMahon**, **Dick Dunkel**, and **Jack Napoli**. McMahon went on to operate as a 5-time public-company CRO — including PTC, Ariba, BladeLogic, and BMC Software (BladeLogic sold to BMC for $872M) — and later joined the boards of Snowflake and MongoDB, carrying the same qualification discipline into those companies' go-to-market leadership.

```
                           THE MEDDPICC FORECAST ENGINE
                           
   M ─── METRICS            What is the quantifiable financial return and economic yield?
   E ─── ECONOMIC BUYER     Who has discretionary spend authority to say YES when others say NO?
   D ─── DECISION CRITERIA  What technical and commercial requirements have we written into their RFP?
   D ─── DECISION PROCESS   What are the exact chronological milestones from selection to board sign-off?
   P ─── PAPER PROCESS      What is the legal, InfoSec, and procurement gauntlet to sign the contract?
   I ─── IDENTIFY PAIN      What is the bleeding Cost of Inaction (COI) that forces executive action?
   C ─── CHAMPION           Who has power, influence, and skin in the game to sell when you aren't there?
   C ─── COMPETITION        Who else is fighting for this capital (vendors, internal tools, status quo)?
```

### The Core Axiom of John McMahon:
> *"Sales reps do not lose deals in the final week of the quarter. They lose deals in the first two weeks of discovery by qualifying with 'happy ears' and falling in love with prospects who have no power."*

If you cannot verify all eight letters of MEDDPICC, you do not have a forecastable opportunity. You have a **wish list item** that will slip, stall, or ghost.

## CHAPTER 2: DECONSTRUCTING THE EIGHT PILLARS

---

### Pillar 1: METRICS (M)
*Metrics represent the quantifiable financial justification for the purchase.*

There are two classes of metrics in B2B SaaS:
1. **M1 (Soft Metrics):** Productivity, collaboration, employee happiness, modern user experience. 
   * *The Problem:* CFOs do not authorize \$50,000 contracts for "better collaboration." During budget reviews, soft metrics are the first to be slashed.
2. **M2 (Hard Economic Metrics):** Direct OpEx reduction, CapEx avoidance, revenue expansion, lease liability reduction, risk mitigation.
   * *The Rule:* A deal is only qualified when you have an **M2 metric verified by the Champion AND confirmed by the Economic Buyer.**

#### The [Your Product] M2 Mathematical Benchmark:
* Leased RSF: 40,000 sq ft.
* Annual Lease Rate: \$48 / RSF / year = \$1,920,000 annual liability.
* Audited Unutilized Peak Capacity: 28% = 11,200 sq ft of dead office space.
* **Quantified M2 Metric:** Eliminating 11,200 RSF on upcoming lease renegotiation saves **\$537,600 annually**.
* **ROI Ratio:** Against a \$36,000 [Your Product] ACV, the hard economic return is **14.9x**.

### Pillar 2: ECONOMIC BUYER (EB)
*The Economic Buyer is the individual with discretionary authority over budget allocation.*

#### The 3 Immutable Rules of the Economic Buyer:
1. **They have veto power:** They can say NO when the entire committee says YES, and they can say YES when everyone else says NO.
2. **They have discretionary spend:** If an unexpected crisis occurs, they can take \$50k allocated for marketing conferences and reallocate it to facilities software without asking for permission.
3. **They speak exclusively in business metrics:** EBs do not care about buttons, screens, or features. They care about EBITDA, risk, corporate liability, headcount efficiency, and shareholder value.

#### How to Expose the False Economic Buyer:
Reps often ask: *"Do you have budget for this?"*  
A junior manager will say *"Yes,"* meaning *"My department has a software budget."* That does NOT make them the Economic Buyer.

The elite qualification question:
> *"If our financial business case proves we can cut \$400k in lease liability, but the software contract exceeds your personal departmental signing authority, who is the executive with the discretionary authority to reallocate funds from real estate capital to software operations?"*

That question forces them to name the real EB: **The CFO, Chief Operating Officer (COO), or VP of Global Real Estate.**

### Pillar 3: DECISION CRITERIA (DC)
*The formal technical, commercial, and operational standards the customer will use to evaluate vendors.*

There are three categories of Decision Criteria:
1. **Technical Criteria:** Security (SOC2 Type II, SSO/SAML, Okta integration), API capabilities, CAD floorplan ingestion speed, mobile responsiveness.
2. **Commercial Criteria:** Pricing model (per desk vs. per employee vs. square footage), contract term flexibility, implementation fees, SLA guarantees.
3. **Operational / Relationship Criteria:** Ease of admin maintenance, customer success support model, vendor viability.

#### The "Trap-Setting" Playbook:
If you receive an RFP or a list of criteria from a customer, you are already losing. That means a competitor got there first and wrote their own architectural strengths into the document.

### Pillar 4: DECISION PROCESS (DP)
*The chronological series of evaluation, validation, and approval milestones from initial contact to vendor selection.*

Reps often confuse *"They liked our demo"* with understanding the Decision Process.

You must map the Decision Process like an engineering workflow:
1. **Technical Validation Stage:** Who evaluates the architectural fit? (Facilities Director + IT Lead).
2. **Business Validation Stage:** Who validates the economic ROI model? (Champion + Director of Finance).
3. **Executive Sponsor Review:** When does the Economic Buyer review the 1-page business case?
4. **Board / Executive Committee Approval:** Does this purchase require sign-off at the monthly executive committee meeting? (If so, what is the submission deadline?).

*The Rookie Trap:* The rep finishes a demo on Oct 20th and says: *"They will sign by Oct 31st!"* But the executive committee only meets on the first Tuesday of every month. The deal cannot close until November 8th at the earliest.

### Pillar 5: PAPER PROCESS (PP)
*The administrative, legal, and procurement gauntlet required to take an agreed-upon decision and convert it into a legally binding, executed contract.*

**This is the #1 reason deals slip past the end of the quarter.** 

Sales reps assume that once a prospect says *"We chose you,"* the contract will sign in 48 hours. In reality, the Paper Process is where deals go to die in bureaucratic purgatory.

```
                           THE 5 RINGS OF THE PAPER PROCESS
                           
   [ 1. INFOSEC & SOC2 ] ──► [ 2. PRIVACY & DPA ] ──► [ 3. LEGAL & MSA ] ──► [ 4. PROCUREMENT ] ──► [ 5. SIGNATURE ]
   120-question security     GDPR, CCPA, and data     Indemnity, liability     Vendor onboarding,   DocuSign queue;
   review by CISO team.      residency audit.         caps, payment terms.     pricing verification. CFO signing threshold.
```

#### How to Map the Paper Process on Day 15 (Not Day 85):
Ask your Champion:
> *"Sarah, congratulations on picking [Your Product] as your preferred platform. To make sure we hit your Nov 15th go-live deadline before your Chicago lease milestone, walk me through your company's actual paper process:*
> 1. *Who on your legal team reviews SaaS Master Services Agreements (MSAs)? Is it internal legal counsel or an outside law firm?*
> 2. *Does your InfoSec team require their own custom security questionnaire, or do they accept our standard SOC2 Type II report and third-party penetration test?*
> 3. *What is your procurement team's vendor onboarding timeline?*
> 4. *Once legal and InfoSec clear the contract, who holds the physical signature authority for a \$35k contract—is it Dave (CFO) directly, or does it require an electronic requisition through Coupa/SAP?"*

If your Champion cannot answer those four questions, **you do not have a close date.** You have a fantasy.

### Pillar 6: IDENTIFIED PAIN (IP)
*The operational friction and financial hemorrhage that makes staying with the status quo intolerable.*

John McMahon defines pain using the acronym **P.A.I.N.**:
* **P - Personal:** How does this problem impact the buyer's reputation, stress level, and career trajectory?
* **A - Acknowledged:** The prospect must admit the problem out loud. (You cannot tell them they have pain; they must say it).
* **I - Implicated:** What downstream business units are being damaged by this pain?
* **N - Numerical:** What is the annual dollar loss or wasted headcount hours resulting from the pain?

If pain is not Numerical, it is not an enterprise pain. It is an annoyance.

### Pillar 7: THE CHAMPION (C)
*An internal stakeholder with power, influence, and personal skin in the game who actively sells on your behalf when you are not in the room.*

#### Coach vs. Champion: The Litmus Test

```
  ATTRIBUTE                     THE COACH                              THE CHAMPION
  ───────────────────────────   ────────────────────────────────────   ──────────────────────────────────────────
  Organizational Standing       Mid-level manager, coordinator.        Senior director, VP, trusted lieutenant.
  Motivation                    Wants software that makes work easy.   Wants career advancement and business ROI.
  Access to the CFO             Has never met the CFO; fears them.     Meets with the CFO regularly; trusted peer.
  Behavior with Competitors     Talks to all vendors equally.          Feeds you competitor intel; guides you.
  Reaction to Hard Requests     Makes excuses; stalls.                 Executes tasks; delivers internal data.
```

#### The 3 Acid Tests to Prove a Champion:
1. **The Intel Test:** Can they explain the exact political landscape, competitor positioning, and budget thresholds?
2. **The Action Test:** Will they pull real building square footage data and employee attendance records for you between calls?
3. **The Access Test:** Will they set up and co-present the business case directly to the Economic Buyer?

*The Rule of Thumb:* If an advocate fails any of these three tests, **they are a Coach.** Do not forecast the deal based on their enthusiasm.

---

### Pillar 8: COMPETITION (COMP)
*Every alternative path the customer can take to solve (or ignore) the problem.*

In enterprise software, your competitors are not just other vendors. There are four types of competition:
1. **Direct Rivals:** Other modern hybrid workspace and desk booking apps (e.g., Envoy, Robin, Kadence).
2. **Legacy IWMS Incumbents:** Massive, bloated enterprise legacy systems already installed at the company (e.g., FM:Systems, Archibus, Planon).
3. **Internal Development / Spreadsheets:** The internal status quo (*"We can just have an intern update our Google Sheets and Outlook calendars"*).
4. **The Ultimate Competitor: DO NOTHING.** The decision to defer the purchase, reallocate budget to another department, and live with the pain.

#### Countering the Status Quo:
You do not defeat the Status Quo by pitching features. You defeat it by **proving the Cost of Inaction (COI)**:
> *"Dave, keeping your current Excel sheets and manual badge swipes costs \$0 on an invoice today. But it is costing your company \$450,000 every single year in unutilized commercial lease liability that you cannot prove or consolidate. Doing nothing is your most expensive option."*

## CHAPTER 3: LIVE MEDDPICC DEAL TEARDOWN
### *The \$75,000 Opportunity Audit*

Let’s review an actual pipeline review between a **VP of Sales (Manager)** and an **Account Executive (Rep)** at [Your Product]:

> **Manager:** "Talk to me about the OmniCorp deal. You have them in the forecast for \$75,000 closing Nov 30th. Are we closing it?"  
> **Rep:** "Yeah, feeling great about it. Mark, the Director of Facilities, is in love with [Your Product]. He told me our interactive floor plans blew away Envoy. He said he has budget and is ready to buy."  
> 
> **Manager:** "Okay, let’s run the MEDDPICC. Who is the Economic Buyer?"  
> **Rep:** "Mark is. He's the Director of Facilities."  
> **Manager:** "Wrong. A Director of Facilities does not have discretionary authority to write a \$75,000 software check without CFO approval. Have you met the CFO?"  
> **Rep:** "No, Mark said he would take the proposal to the CFO himself."  
> 
> **Manager:** "Red flag number one. Mark is a Coach, not a Champion. Have you tested Mark with a hard request? Did he give you their actual lease agreements or floorplan square-footage CAD drawings?"  
> **Rep:** "He said he’d try to find them after we send pricing."  
> 
> **Manager:** "Red flag number two. He hasn't done any work for you. Now, what about the Paper Process? Have you initiated InfoSec review?"  
> **Rep:** "We’ll send the security questionnaire once they agree to pricing."  
> 
> **Manager:** "OmniCorp is a 2,000-person public financial services company. Their InfoSec review takes a minimum of 4 weeks. Their legal team uses an outside firm in New York that redlines every limitation of liability clause for 3 weeks. If you haven't started InfoSec on Nov 2nd, there is a **0.0% chance this deal closes by Nov 30th.**  
> 
> Pull this deal out of the commit forecast immediately. It's in the pipeline, but it is totally unqualified."

#### The Prescriptive Action Plan to Save the Deal:
1. **Test Mark immediately:** Tell Mark that before pricing can be released, [Your Product]’s executive solution team requires a 20-minute financial scoping call with him and the CFO.
2. **If Mark refuses:** Mark is an unqualified Coach. The rep must multi-thread directly to the VP of Corporate Real Estate or CFO using lease-consolidation messaging.
3. **Trigger the Paper Process early:** Send the standard SOC2 Type II package to OmniCorp’s IT gatekeeper immediately to run in parallel with discovery.

## CHAPTER 4: THE MEDDPICC DEAL SCORING RUBRIC

Use this empirical 16-point scorecard to evaluate any active enterprise deal:

| Letter | Factor | 0 Points (Unknown) | 1 Point (Fragile / In Progress) | 2 Points (Confirmed / Locked) |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **M** | **Metrics** | No hard dollar figures discussed. | Soft productivity metrics discussed with champion. | Hard M2 lease/OpEx savings confirmed in writing by CFO. |
| **E** | **Economic Buyer** | Have never spoken to or identified EB. | EB identified, but rep has not met them directly. | Direct 1-on-1 meeting held with EB; metrics verified. |
| **D** | **Decision Criteria** | Customer has not defined criteria. | Customer provided criteria; aligns somewhat. | Rep helped write criteria; trap doors planted against rivals. |
| **D** | **Decision Process** | Timeline is based on rep's guess. | High-level milestones discussed. | Exact dates, committee review meetings, and owners mapped. |
| **P** | **Paper Process** | Legal and InfoSec have not been contacted. | InfoSec questionnaire submitted. | Legal counsel, MSA redlines, and signature thresholds mapped. |
| **I** | **Identified Pain** | Surface-level complaints (*"tools are clunky"*). | Operational problem acknowledged by facilities. | Numerical Cost of Inaction (\$ loss) admitted by executive. |
| **C** | **Champion** | Friendly contact with no organizational power. | Advocate likes us but hasn't passed access test. | Tested power-broker who actively sells for us to the EB. |
| **C** | **Competition** | Unaware of who else is being evaluated. | Competitor names known, but no strategy deployed. | Clear displacement strategy executed; traps sprung. |

### Scoring Assessment:
* **14–16 Points:** **High Probability Commit.** High likelihood of closing on or ahead of schedule.
* **10–13 Points:** **Upside / Fluff Pipeline.** High risk of close-date slippage. Requires immediate Champion testing and executive access.
* **Under 10 Points:** **Unqualified Risk.** Do not forecast. Single-threaded and highly vulnerable to ghosting.
