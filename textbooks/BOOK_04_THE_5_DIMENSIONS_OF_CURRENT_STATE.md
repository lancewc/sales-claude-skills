## EXECUTIVE PREFACE: WHY SURFACE DISCOVERY DESTROYS CLOSING RATES
Most sales discovery calls are shallow interrogations. An inexperienced rep asks: *"What software are you currently using? What do you like about it? What is your budget?"* The prospect provides short, superficial answers, and the rep immediately jumps into a demo.

In **Keenan's GAP Selling methodology**, discovery is not about checking boxes. It is an MRI of the customer's operational reality. Selling only happens when there is a quantifiable distance—a **GAP**—between the prospect's **Current State** and their desired **Future State**.

If an Account Executive does not completely map the Current State across all 5 dimensions, they cannot measure the GAP. Without a measurable GAP, there is no urgency, no value, and no budget.

---

## METHODOLOGY GROUNDING: WHAT KEENAN'S GAP SELLING ACTUALLY SAYS ABOUT CURRENT STATE

Keenan's 2018 book *Gap Selling: Getting the Customer to Yes* (Sales Gravy Press) is the source of this framework, and it's worth being precise about what the book itself argues versus what this volume adds on top of it — sloppy attribution is exactly the kind of "check-the-box" thinking GAP Selling exists to kill.

In Keenan's own terms, every deal has two poles: the **Current State** (where the customer is today — including their problems and the root causes behind them) and the **Future State** (where they want to be, including the technical, business, and competitive improvements that would follow). The **Gap** is the measurable, monetized distance between the two, and that Gap — not the product — is what the customer is actually buying. Keenan is explicit that discovery's job is not requirements-gathering; it's building what he calls a **Problem Identification Chart**: for every problem, document its impact and its root cause until you understand the customer's business better than they've articulated it to themselves.

He also breaks discovery questions into four distinct types, not one undifferentiated "discovery call": **probing questions** (open-ended questions that map the Current State), **process questions** (how things actually happen day to day, not how the org chart claims they happen), **provocation questions** (questions that challenge an assumption or surface a cost the prospect hasn't priced out), and **validation questions** (questions that confirm your understanding is correct before you move forward).

The "5 Dimensions" structure used in this volume — Physical, Technical, Business, Financial, Emotional — is **not a verbatim table of contents from Keenan's book**. It is this program's operational breakdown of the same territory he insists reps must cover before they are allowed to talk about a solution: the literal facts of the environment, the systems layered on top of it, the business processes that break because of it, the dollars it costs, and the personal and political stakes for the human being across the table. Treat the 5 Dimensions as a disciplined checklist for executing Keenan's Current State mapping in the field — not as a direct quote from the source material. If you tell a prospect "this is straight out of the GAP Selling book," be ready to describe it accurately: Current State, Future State, the Gap, and the Problem Identification Chart are Keenan's terms; the 5-Dimension breakdown is this program's operationalization of them.

## CHAPTER 1: DECONSTRUCTING THE 5 DIMENSIONS OF CURRENT STATE

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE 5 DIMENSIONS OF THE CURRENT STATE                │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. THE PHYSICAL / LITERAL FACTS                                        │
│ • Exact square footage, headcount, lease dates, desk counts, tools.   │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. THE TECHNICAL ENVIRONMENT                                           │
│ • Badge systems, calendar platforms, SSO, ERP, CAD mapping software.   │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. THE BUSINESS PROBLEMS (The "So What?")                              │
│ • What literal business processes are breaking down due to the setup?  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. THE FINANCIAL IMPACT (The Quantified Bleed)                         │
│ • Wasted lease expense, overtime payroll, delayed hiring, lost revenue.│
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. THE EMOTIONAL IMPACT (The Personal Pain)                            │
│ • Stress, executive embarrassment, fear of career failure, burnout.    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Dimension 1: The Physical & Literal Facts
These are indisputable, verifiable data points:
- *"How many total physical offices do you manage?"*
- *"What is the exact square footage of the Chicago headquarters?"*
- *"How many total badge holders vs. dedicated desks exist today?"*
- *"When does the current lease expire?"*

### Dimension 2: The Technical Infrastructure
How technology interacts with the physical space:
- *"Which identity provider controls user provisioning (Azure AD, Okta)?"*
- *"Are you currently managing floor plans on static AutoCAD files, PDFs, or Visio?"*
- *"How do employees reserve meeting spaces today (Outlook, Google, physical paper)?"*

### Dimension 3: The Business Problem
Where the friction occurs:
- Employees drive 45 minutes to the office only to find no available desks near their project team.
- Department heads fight over conference rooms because sales reps camp in executive boardrooms for phone calls.
- Facilities coordinators spend 15 hours every week manually redrawing seating charts when teams restructure.

### Dimension 4: The Financial Impact
Monetizing the business problem into dollars and cents:
- 15 hours/week of coordinator time at $40/hr = $31,200/year in administrative waste.
- 18,000 square feet of ghost desks at $42/sq ft = $756,000/year in unutilized lease burn.
- 4 engineering resignations cited lack of quiet collaboration space = $160,000 in recruiting replacement costs.

### Dimension 5: The Emotional Impact
Enterprise purchases are justified with logic, but initiated by emotion:
- The VP of Facilities is terrified that the upcoming executive board meeting will expose their complete lack of utilization data.
- The Chief People Officer is exhausted from receiving complaints on Slack about office overcrowding on Wednesdays.

## CHAPTER 2: GONG CALL TRANSCRIPT: DISSECTING CURRENT STATE
*Context: AE [Your Name] interviewing Greg, Senior Director of Real Estate at an AmLaw 100 Law Firm in Chicago.*

> **[Your Name] (AE):** "Greg, to understand whether we can even help you, walk me through how your 450 partners and associates interact with your three floors on Michigan Avenue today."
>
> **Greg (Director):** "Well, right now partners have dedicated corner offices, but associates are supposed to share cubicles. People use an Excel spreadsheet on SharePoint to claim desks, but nobody updates it. It's a bit chaotic on Tuesdays."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "When you say 'it's a bit chaotic,' what does that actually mean when an associate walks through the turnstile on Tuesday morning?" *(Probing Dimension 1 & 3)*
>
> **Greg (Director):** "Associates show up, find out someone else took the desk they put on Excel, and they end up sitting in the cafeteria or going home angry. Then the managing partner calls me asking why his litigation team isn't sitting together."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "Understood. When the managing partner calls you angry about his team, how often is that happening, and how much of your day does resolving that absorb?" *(Probing Dimension 3 & 5)*
>
> **Greg (Director):** "At least twice a week. I'm spending 6 to 8 hours a week playing referee instead of managing our lease renewals."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "And while you're refereeing seating charts, your lease on the 14th floor is up for renewal in 9 months. What is that 14th floor costing the partnership annually?" *(Probing Dimension 4)*
>
> **Greg (Director):** "That floor is $920,000 a year."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "So right now, you are spending $920k on a floor where half the desks sit empty on Mondays and Fridays, associates are fighting over seats on Tuesdays, and you're spending a full working day every week refereeing Slack complaints. Is that an accurate picture of where things stand?"
>
> **Greg (Director):** "When you summarize it like that, it sounds insane. Yes, that is exactly where we are."

## CHAPTER 3: THE 5-DIMENSION CURRENT STATE AUDIT WORKSHEET
Before demonstrating any product feature, ensure your CRM opportunity notes contain explicit answers for each:

1. **Physical Facts:** Square footage, desk count, lease expiration date, peak in-office days.
2. **Technical Facts:** Calendar software, badge hardware, directory sync method.
3. **Root Business Problem:** The primary breakdown in daily operations.
4. **Financial Impact ($):** Annualized real estate waste + labor overhead.
5. **Emotional Consequence:** The buyer's personal status, frustration, or vulnerability.

## CHAPTER 4: SECOND CALL TEARDOWN — A DIFFERENT VERTICAL, THE SAME 5 DIMENSIONS

*Context: AE [Your Name] on a discovery call with Priya, VP of People & Workplace at a 650-person software company that just mandated three in-office days per week.*

> **[Your Name] (AE):** "Priya, before we talk about any software, help me understand what actually happens at your two buildings on a Tuesday. How many employees, how many desks, and how are those desks currently assigned?" *(Dimension 1: Physical)*
>
> **Priya (VP People):** "We have about 650 employees between the two buildings, roughly 500 desks total since we planned for hybrid. Desks aren't assigned — it's first-come, first-served."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "Got it. And today, how does someone actually find or claim a desk when they walk in? What's the tool, if any?" *(Dimension 2: Technical)*
>
> **Priya (VP People):** "Honestly, nothing formal. People just walk the floor and grab whatever's open. A few teams built their own tracker in a shared Google Sheet."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "When someone can't find an open desk near their team, what actually happens next?" *(Dimension 3: Business Problem)*
>
> **Priya (VP People):** "They end up scattered — an engineer sits three floors from the rest of their pod, or in the cafeteria with a laptop. Standups become half in-person, half shouting across a room. Managers have started telling people to just work from home instead, which defeats the entire point of the policy."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "So the in-office mandate is quietly failing because the space itself can't execute it. Do you have a sense of how much that's costing — not in software, in actual dollars?" *(Dimension 4: Financial Impact)*
>
> **Priya (VP People):** "Our CFO ran the math when he approved the mandate: getting people back in-office was supposed to protect about $2.1M a year in onboarding and collaboration losses we saw during full-remote. If the policy isn't actually working because people can't sit together, we're not capturing any of that $2.1M — we're just paying commute stipends for nothing."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "That's a real number. Last question, and I ask this because it matters: how is this landing on you personally? You're the one who has to report back on whether the mandate is working." *(Dimension 5: Emotional Impact)*
>
> **Priya (VP People):** "It's landing badly. I'm the one who told our CEO this policy would work. Every all-hands, someone asks me publicly why they still can't sit with their team. I'm starting to feel like I own a failure I didn't actually cause."
>
> **[Your Name] (AE):** "So to summarize: 650 people, a policy your CEO is watching, $2.1M in projected value that isn't materializing because there's no system to seat teams together, and you're the one absorbing the heat for it in every all-hands. Is that a fair picture?"
>
> **Priya (VP People):** "Painfully fair. Yes."

## CHAPTER 5: WHERE REPS GET THIS WRONG

Knowing the 5 Dimensions exist is not the same as executing them. The most common failure patterns:

1. **Stopping at Dimensions 1–2 and calling it discovery.** Reps who only gather physical and technical facts have completed an inventory, not a Current State diagnosis. Without Dimensions 3–5, there is no monetized Gap — only a features conversation waiting to happen.
2. **Doing the math for the prospect instead of eliciting it from them.** Dimension 4 only works if the *buyer* says the number, in their own words. An AE who shows up with a pre-built ROI slide instead of asking "what is that costing you?" hands the buyer a number to argue with in procurement instead of a number they personally own.
3. **Treating Dimension 5 as manipulation.** GAP Selling's emotional dimension is not about exploiting fear — it's about surfacing a real, already-existing personal stake and reflecting it back accurately. If you find yourself inventing anxiety the buyer hasn't expressed, you've left diagnosis and entered manipulation. Mirror their words; don't supply your own.
4. **Running the dimensions as a disconnected checklist instead of a single narrative.** Each answer should set up the next question. When a rep jumps from square footage straight to "how does this make you feel," the seams show and the call feels like a survey, not a conversation.
5. **Skipping validation before advancing.** Moving from Dimension 1 to Dimension 2 without confirming the buyer agrees with your summary of the facts creates disputes mid-demo, when it's most expensive to discover you misunderstood something basic.
6. **Reading the dimensions as a script.** The words above are illustrations, not a teleprompter. A rep who asks all five questions verbatim, in order, on every call sounds like a form — and prospects who've been "GAP Sold" at badly before will notice.
