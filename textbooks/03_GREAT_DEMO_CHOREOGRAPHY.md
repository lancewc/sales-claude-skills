## CHAPTER 1: THE FATAL FLAW OF SAAS DEMONSTRATIONS

In *Great Demo!*, Peter Cohan exposes the single most destructive habit in modern B2B software sales: **The Harbor Tour.**

Reps open Zoom, share their screen, and treat the prospective buyer like a new employee on their first day of software training:
1. They show the login page and explain Single Sign-On.
2. They tour the navigation sidebar, explaining what every icon does.
3. They open the administrative settings menu and show how user permissions are configured.
4. They click into sample data sets and demonstrate mundane button mechanics.
5. In minute 38, after the executive has already checked out or started answering emails, the rep finally reaches the high-level reporting dashboard.

```
       TRADITIONAL "HARBOR TOUR" DEMO                    PETER COHAN'S "GREAT DEMO!"
               (Linear & Boring)                              (Inverted Pyramid)
  
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐     ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ Min 01: Login, Settings, Navigation    │     │ MIN 01–03: THE PAYOFF DASHBOARD        │
   │────────────────────────────────────────│     │            (Do the Last Thing First)   │
   │ Min 10: User setup, permissions, forms │     ├────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │────────────────────────────────────────│     │ Min 04–15: THE CRITICAL FEW WORKFLOWS  │
   │ Min 20: Button mechanics, data entry   │     │            (Only what drives the delta)│
   │────────────────────────────────────────│     ├────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ Min 35: Finally show the report/value  │     │ Min 16–30: COMMERCIAL ALIGNMENT & NEXT │
   │ (Executive has already stopped caring) │     │            (Co-authoring the MAP)      │
   └────────────────────────────────────────┘     └────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Why Harbor Tours Murder Win Rates:
* **Attention Spans Are Decaying:** Cognitive science proves that human attention peaks in the first 4 minutes of a presentation and steadily declines thereafter. If you save your best screen for the end, you are presenting your highest-value capability to an audience whose brains have turned off.
* **Showing Plumbing Triggers Adoption Anxiety:** Every time you click an admin settings screen, open a configuration dropdown, or show complex setup steps, the customer thinks: *"This tool looks complicated. My employees will never figure this out. This is going to be an onboarding nightmare."*
* **You Invite Feature Nitpicking:** When you show 40 features that the customer didn't ask for, you give them 40 new reasons to find flaws, compare you to a cheaper rival, or raise unnecessary technical objections.

## CHAPTER 2: THE INVERTED PYRAMID METHOD
### *"Do the Last Thing First"*

In journalism, reporters use the Inverted Pyramid: the headline and the most critical facts are placed in the very first paragraph, followed by supporting details, with trivial background info at the bottom.

In software sales, you must **Do the Last Thing First**.

You open the software demonstration directly to the **final payoff screen**—the exact dashboard, report, or visual outcome that solves the primary financial pain uncovered in discovery.

#### The 3-Minute Payoff Script ([Your Product] Executive Demonstration)
*Attendees: Dave (CFO) and Sarah (Workplace Director).*

> **AE:** *"Dave, Sarah, thanks for hopping on. On Tuesday, we discussed how your upcoming Chicago lease renewal is putting \$4.5M on the line, and right now you have zero empirical data to know if you can safely terminate Floor 3 without running out of desks.*
> 
> *Instead of walking you through our user settings or how an admin sets up a profile, I want to show you the final screen your executive committee will look at every single Monday morning.*
> 
> *(AE shares screen directly to the [Your Product] Executive Spatial Utilization Analytics Dashboard)*
> 
> *Dave, look right here in the center of your screen:*
> * *Over the last 90 days, your peak hybrid attendance across your 450 Chicago employees occurred on Wednesdays, averaging 64% occupancy.*
> * *On Mondays and Fridays, occupancy drops to 21%.*
> * *More importantly, look at Floor 3: peak occupancy on Floor 3 has never exceeded 31% over the last two quarters.*
> 
> *This single screen gives your finance team the mathematical proof that you can collapse Floor 3, move those teams into dynamic neighborhoods on Floors 1 and 2, and cut **\$900,000 a year** out of your commercial lease liability with zero desk shortages.*
> 
> *Let me pause right there. Dave, if you had this exact data in your board packet next month, how does that change your negotiation with your landlord?"*

#### What Happens in the Buyer's Brain?
1. **The CFO is instantly locked in.** You answered their primary business question in 120 seconds.
2. **The context of the software is anchored to \$900k.** Every button you show after this is viewed as an instrument to capture that \$900k savings.
3. **You have earned the right to show the mechanics.** Now the buyer will lean in and ask: *"How does the software generate that data? Show me how employees interact with it."*

## CHAPTER 3: THE FEWEST CLICKS TO VALUE

Every single mouse click during a software demo carries a cognitive and commercial cost.

### The Cohan Click Laws:
1. **Pre-populate all demo environments:** Never type in sample names, passwords, department names, or floor numbers live during a sales call. It makes you look amateurish and creates dead air.
2. **The 3-Click Rule:** Any workflow you demonstrate must reach its value payoff within 3 clicks. If it takes 9 clicks, you must script a shortcut or present the completed state first.
3. **Never touch the Settings icon:** Banish the gear icon from your demos. Settings are for post-sale implementation training, not sales demonstrations.

```
   ROOKIE DEMO FLOW (11 Clicks of Friction):
   Click Admin ──► Click Settings ──► Click Workspaces ──► Click Floor 2 ──► Click "Add Zone" 
   ──► Type "Marketing" ──► Select Color Blue ──► Click Save ──► Refresh Screen...
   
   ELITE GREAT DEMO! FLOW (1 Click of Value):
   Open directly to the pre-configured Marketing Neighborhood. 
   Highlight the 20 desks with a single click: 
   "Notice how Marketing is dynamically zoned with automated permissions in one tap."
```

## CHAPTER 4: THE CRITICAL FEW VS. THE NON-CRITICAL MANY

Your product engineering team works hard to ship 50 new features every quarter. Your marketing team writes release notes celebrating every toggle and integration.

**The prospect does not care.**

To execute an elite demo, you must ruthlessly separate the **Critical Few** from the **Non-Critical Many**:
* **The Critical Few (The 20%):** The 2 or 3 specific workflows that directly prove your solution solves the quantified business bleed uncovered in discovery.
* **The Non-Critical Many (The 80%):** The 40 secondary features (custom profile photos, emoji reactions on desk check-in, parking space reservations, visitor badge printing).

#### The Law of Presentation Discipline:
If a feature does not directly connect to a problem the customer admitted having during discovery, **do not show it.**

If you show a secondary feature unprompted, you invite the **Feature Distraction Trap**:
> *Rep unprompted shows parking space booking feature.*  
> *Buyer:* "Oh, does this integrate with our specific gate RFID system at our Austin garage?"  
> *Rep:* "Uh, no, it's a mobile QR scan."  
> *Buyer:* "Ah, that won't work for us. Our union employees won't scan QR codes for parking. Let's hold off on software until you support RFID."  

You just stalled a \$50,000 real estate deal over an irrelevant parking feature that the customer wasn't even looking for.

## CHAPTER 5: HOW TO HANDLE QUESTIONS DURING A DEMO
### *Illustrations vs. Demos (The TelSell Rule)*

During a demo, prospects will frequently ask technical or hypothetical questions:
* *"Can we export this floorplan data into an Excel spreadsheet or PowerBI?"*
* *"What happens if an employee books a desk and doesn't show up?"*
* *"Can an executive assistant book a desk on behalf of someone else?"*

Amateur reps treat every question as an invitation to go on a 10-minute feature detour. They stop their narrative, click into three different menus, and derail their own presentation.

Peter Cohan introduces the distinction between an **Illustration** and a **Demo**:

| Tool | Purpose | Duration | Structure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Illustration** | Answers a specific question quickly without derailing the meeting. | 30 to 60 seconds | **Tell $\rightarrow$ Show $\rightarrow$ Check.** Show one screen, verify satisfaction, and return immediately to the primary value arc. |
| **Demo** | Proves a core business use-case tied to primary discovery pain. | 5 to 10 minutes | **Payoff first $\rightarrow$ Fewest clicks workflow $\rightarrow$ Delta quantification.** |

#### The 3-Step "TelSell" Illustration Technique:
1. **Step 1: Tell (Direct Answer):** *"Yes, absolutely. The system has automated desk release rules."*
2. **Step 2: Show (1-Screen Proof):** Switch to the notification screen: *"If an employee doesn't check in within 30 minutes of their reservation, the desk automatically turns green on the map and releases to the neighborhood."*
3. **Step 3: Check (Regain Control):** *"Does that give your team the automated ghost-booking protection you were looking for?"*  
   *Buyer:* "Yes, perfect."  
   *Rep:* *"Great, let's jump right back to our Chicago lease consolidation model."*

You answered the question, proved the capability, and returned to your value narrative in 45 seconds without surrendering conversational control.

## CHAPTER 6: THE SCRIPTED 15-MINUTE EXECUTIVE DEMO FLOW
### *[Your Product] Enterprise Presentation Choreography*

* **Total Scheduled Time:** 30 Minutes
* **Total Demo Screen-Share Time:** Exactly 14 Minutes
* **Remaining Time (16 Minutes):** Business Discussion, Champion Alignment, and Mutual Action Plan (MAP) Execution.

```
   [ MINUTE 00–02 ]  Agenda Alignment & Re-anchoring Discovery Pain
   [ MINUTE 02–05 ]  The Executive Payoff Screen (Doing the Last Thing First: Real Estate Analytics)
   [ MINUTE 05–09 ]  Workflow 1: Employee Self-Service (Neighborhood Desk Booking in 2 Clicks)
   [ MINUTE 09–13 ]  Workflow 2: Facilities Move Management (Drag-and-Drop Floorplan Scenarios)
   [ MINUTE 13–14 ]  Re-closing the Loop on the Business Delta ($900k Annual Lease Savings)
   [ MINUTE 14–30 ]  Screen-Share OFF: Transition to Mutual Action Plan & Next Steps
```

### Verbatim Script Breakdown:

#### Minute 00–02: Re-anchoring the Discovery Pain
> *"Dave, Sarah, before I share my screen, let's make sure we are 100% aligned on why we're here today.  
> On our discovery call, you shared that your hybrid attendance averages 64% on peak days and 21% on Mondays, and you are 14 months away from a \$4.5M lease renewal on your Chicago HQ. You need empirical occupancy data to prove whether you can terminate Floor 3 without creating desk shortages. Is that still our primary focus today?"*  
> *(They agree).*

#### Minute 02–05: The Payoff Screen (Doing the Last Thing First)
> *(Share screen directly to Executive Spatial Analytics).*  
> *"Here is the payoff: an automated real-time view showing your executive committee that Floor 3 runs at 28% peak utilization, proving you can consolidate those heads onto Floors 1 and 2, saving \$900,000 annually."*

#### Minute 05–09: Workflow 1 (Employee Experience in 2 Clicks)
> *"Now, how does that data get generated without annoying your employees? Watch this: an employee opens [Your Product] on their phone or inside Slack, clicks their department neighborhood, sees where their manager is sitting, and taps 'Reserve'. That took 4 seconds. No forms, no confusion."*

#### Minute 09–13: Workflow 2 (Facilities In-House Move Management)
> *"And Sarah, for your facilities team who used to spend 6 hours in Visio: watch how you reconfigure space. You click 'Scenario Planner', highlight 20 desks on Floor 3, drag them into the west wing of Floor 2, and click 'Apply'. The floor plan is live, rosters are updated, and employees are notified. A 6-hour manual headache just became a 60-second in-house workflow."*

#### Minute 13–14: Re-closing the Loop
> *"That is [Your Product] in a nutshell: 4-second booking for employees, 60-second floorplan agility for facilities, and \$900,000 in lease savings for Dave's finance team."*

#### Minute 14–30: Screen-Share OFF (The Power Move)
> *(AE stops screen share. The faces of the attendees fill the screen).*  
> *"Let's take down the screen. Dave, Sarah—based on what you've seen, does this provide the operational proof you need to execute your Chicago consolidation plan?"*

*Notice the power move:* Turning off the screen share in minute 14 stops the prospect from staring at software buttons and forces them into a face-to-face executive conversation about budget, timeline, and next steps.
