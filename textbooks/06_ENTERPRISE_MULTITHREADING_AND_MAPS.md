## EXECUTIVE PREFACE: THE SINGLE-THREADED GRAVEYARD
In enterprise B2B sales ($30,000 to $250,000+ ACV), single-threaded deals are dead on arrival. Gartner’s enterprise buying study shows that the typical B2B purchase involves **6.8 to 10 decision-makers**, each representing distinct departmental agendas, risk profiles, and veto powers. 

When an AE relies on a single contact—even if that contact has a "VP" title—the deal is vulnerable to four inevitable corporate hazards:
1. **The Ghosting Hazard:** The contact gets overwhelmed by quarterly priorities and goes silent.
2. **The Promotion/Departure Hazard:** The contact switches jobs or gets reorganized, wiping out months of discovery.
3. **The Internal Pitch Hazard:** The contact attempts to pitch your solution to the CFO or CEO without your domain mastery, fails to quantify ROI, and gets denied.
4. **The Shadow Blocker Hazard:** An unconsulted stakeholder (Information Security, Workplace IT, or Procurement) blocks the purchase in final contract stages.

This volume provides the complete operational blueprint for **Enterprise Multi-Threading**, **Stakeholder Triangulation**, and **Mutual Action Plan (MAP) Execution**.

## CHAPTER 1: THE 4 ENTERPRISE BUYER PERSONAS
Every enterprise purchase involves four distinct functional roles. Elite AEs never pitch until they have identified and mapped the individual occupying each chair:

```

│                   THE 4 ENTERPRISE BUYER ARCHETYPES                    │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. THE ECONOMIC BUYER (EB)     │ 2. THE CHAMPION                       │
│ • Holds discretionary budget   │ • Possesses the pain & power to sell  │
│ • Obsessed with EBITDA, cash   │ • Willing to stake personal capital   │
│ • Metric: Net ROI, Risk, Payback│ • Metric: Operational relief, status │
├────────────────────────────────┤───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. THE INFLUENCER / USER       │ 4. THE SHADOW BLOCKER                 │
│ • Lives in the workflows daily │ • Information Security, Legal, CRE    │
│ • Cares about UX & simplicity  │ • Focused on risk, compliance, specs  │
│ • Metric: Daily friction cut   │ • Metric: Zero breach, vendor terms   │
└────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1. The Economic Buyer (EB)
- **Who they are:** Chief Financial Officer (CFO), Chief Operating Officer (COO), or Executive Vice President (EVP).
- **Their Mindset:** They do not care about software features. They view every dollar spent through the lens of capital allocation: *"Does spending \$50,000 on software yield more enterprise value than hiring two engineers or buying short-term Treasury bills?"*
- **How to engage:** Never ask them about their daily workflows. Approach them with macro-economic metrics: lease liabilities, EBITDA expansion, and capital expenditure avoidance.

### 2. The Champion
- **Who they are:** VP of Real Estate, VP of People, or Senior Director of Workplace Experience.
- **The McMahon Test:** A true Champion must pass three criteria:
  1. They have personal pain that your software solves.
  2. They have internal influence and access to the Economic Buyer.
  3. They give you proprietary inside information (budget cycles, competitor bids, legal redlines).
- **Warning:** If someone loves your product but cannot get you a 20-minute meeting with the CFO, they are a **Friendly Coach**, not a Champion.

### 3. The Influencer / Power User
- **Who they are:** Workplace Coordinators, Facilities Managers, IT Helpdesk Leads.
- **Their Mindset:** *"Will this make my Tuesday morning easier, or will it create 200 Slack tickets from angry employees?"*
- **How to engage:** Involve them early to validate ease of implementation and SSO integration. If ignored, they will quietly tell the VP: *"Our current system is fine, this new tool looks complicated."*

### 4. The Shadow Blocker (Security, IT & Legal)
- **Who they are:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), Corporate Counsel, Procurement Director.
- **Their Mindset:** Risk mitigation. Their entire job performance is graded on preventing data leaks, avoiding GDPR/SOC2 compliance penalties, and negotiating net-60 payment terms.
- **How to engage:** **Never wait until Stage 4 to introduce Security and Legal.** Request their Vendor Assessment Questionnaire and Master Services Agreement (MSA) during the initial discovery phase:
  > *"Sarah, most enterprise rollouts get delayed 4 weeks in legal and InfoSec. Let's pull in your IT security lead on Tuesday so we can clear their SOC2 Type II requirements while you and I validate the commercial business case."*

## CHAPTER 2: STAKEHOLDER TRIANGULATION IN WORKPLACE SAAS
When selling Maptician or enterprise workplace software, you must triangulate value across four conflicting corporate departments:

| Department | Primary Metric / Concern | The Value Proposition Pitch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Corporate Real Estate (CRE)** | Lease cost/sq ft, lease expiration dates, space utilization rates. | *"We deliver the real-time presence data that lets you safely shed 20% of leased square footage at your upcoming Q4 renewal."* |
| **Human Resources / People Ops** | Employee retention, commute sentiment, team collaboration, friction-free desk booking. | *"We eliminate employee desk hunting anxiety and enable cross-functional team neighborhood clustering without forcing rigid mandates."* |
| **Information Technology (IT)** | SSO/SAML 2.0, SCIM provisioning, calendar syncing (Google Workspace/Office 365), IoT sensor security. | *"We deploy in 72 hours via native Azure AD SCIM integration with zero hardware gateway dependencies and complete SOC2 Type II compliance."* |
| **Finance / CFO** | OpEx reduction, cash flow payback period, EBITDA impact. | *"We cannibalize a \$2.4M lease liability with a \$35k software investment, producing positive ROI in under 45 days."* |

## CHAPTER 3: THE MUTUAL ACTION PLAN (MAP) PROTOCOL
The **Mutual Action Plan (MAP)**—also known as a Joint Execution Plan (JEP)—is the single most effective closing instrument in enterprise software. It transforms a one-sided sales chase into a collaborative, mutual project plan agreed upon with the customer.

### The Rule of the Anchor Event
A MAP is never anchored to *"When do we sign the contract?"* Buyers do not care about signing contracts; they care about their business go-live date.
- **Wrong Anchor:** *"Can we get signatures by September 30th for our end of quarter?"* (Selfish, rookie AE).
- **Right Anchor:** *"Sarah, you stated you must announce the new hybrid office seating policy on November 1st before the Dallas lease terminates on December 31st. To hit that November 1st launch safely, here is the milestone schedule working backward from that date."*

### Verbatim 8-Stage MAP Architecture:

```
MILESTONE 1: Commercial Business Case & ROI Model Validation
• Target Date: Week 1
• Owner: AE + Champion (VP Real Estate)
• Deliverable: 1-Page CFO Business Case and lease savings projection approved.

MILESTONE 2: Technical & Security Architecture Review
• Target Date: Week 2
• Owner: Sales Engineer + Enterprise IT/InfoSec
• Deliverable: SOC2 Type II, SAML SSO, and SCIM provisioning verified.

MILESTONE 3: Economic Buyer Executive Briefing
• Target Date: Week 3
• Owner: AE + Champion + CFO
• Deliverable: CFO greenlights budget allocation based on EBITDA expansion.

MILESTONE 4: Legal & MSA Redline Exchange
• Target Date: Week 4
• Owner: General Counsel (Vendor) + Corporate Legal (Buyer)
• Deliverable: Standard Master Services Agreement and DPA finalized.

MILESTONE 5: Procurement & Order Form Signoff
• Target Date: Week 5
• Owner: Procurement Lead + VP Real Estate
• Deliverable: Executed Order Form and Purchase Order (PO) issued.

MILESTONE 6: Tenant Provisioning & Floor Plan Digitization
• Target Date: Week 6
• Owner: Implementation Lead + Facilities Coordinator
• Deliverable: CAD drawings ingested, floor maps published.

MILESTONE 7: Departmental Pilot & Champion Testing
• Target Date: Week 7
• Owner: HR Lead + Pilot User Group
• Deliverable: Mobile desk booking and neighborhood zones verified.

MILESTONE 8: Company-Wide Go-Live & Lease Termination
• Target Date: Week 8 (November 1st)
• Owner: Executive Leadership Team
• Deliverable: Hybrid office fully operational; excess lease surrendered.
```

## CHAPTER 4: CONTROLLING THE PAPER PROCESS
John McMahon teaches that more deals die in the **Paper Process** than in technical evaluation. 

The Paper Process encompasses every administrative step required to turn a verbal *"yes"* into a signed, countersigned, legally binding agreement:
1. **Who has signature authority?** In many enterprises, a VP can approve an invoice up to \$25,000, but any multi-year contract or agreement over \$50,000 requires the CFO, President, or Board of Directors.
2. **Is a Purchase Order (PO) required?** If an AE gets a DocuSign signed but Accounts Payable requires a PO number before releasing funds, the deal cannot be booked.
3. **What is the vendor onboarding timeline?** Large enterprises use platforms like Coupa, SAP Ariba, or Zip. Onboarding a new vendor into Coupa can take 3 to 6 weeks.
4. **Who handles InfoSec and DPA?** If the customer's legal team insists on redlining the Data Processing Agreement (DPA) regarding employee names and emails, this can freeze a deal for 30 days unless pre-empted.

### The AE Paper Process Discovery Script:
> *"Sarah, congratulations on selecting Maptician. To make sure we don't encounter surprises that delay your November 1st launch, let's walk through the mechanics of how contracts are executed at your company:*
> 1. *Who is the authorized signatory for software agreements over \$30k?*
> 2. *Does your legal team use standard vendor agreements, or will you require us to sign on your paper?*
> 3. *Does your procurement team require vendor registration in Coupa or Ariba?*
> 4. *Once contracts are fully executed, does Accounts Payable require a separate Purchase Order before processing?"*

## CHAPTER 5: FIELD EXERCISES & ROLEPLAYS

### Drill 1: The Multi-Threading Pivot
- **Scenario:** You have had two great discovery calls with the Workplace Experience Manager. She says: *"I love the product! Send me a quote and I will show it to our CFO next week."*
- **Your Task:** Do not send the quote. Script the pivot that secures a joint executive briefing with the CFO.
- **Elite Script:**
  > *"Jennifer, I'm thrilled this resonates. Here is what typically happens: CFOs review software purely on capital allocation, balance sheet lease liabilities, and EBITDA impact. If I just send you a quote, you'll be forced to defend our ROI metrics without the financial models we built for companies like yours. Let's do this: let's jump on a 20-minute working session with your CFO, where you and I co-present the space consolidation business case together. How does Thursday at 2 PM look for her calendar?"*

### Drill 2: The Security Pre-Emption
- **Scenario:** The prospect says: *"We love the demo. Let's do a pilot first, and then we will look at security and contracts."*
- **Your Task:** Defend the sales cycle from the dreaded 6-week post-pilot security freeze.
- **Elite Script:**
  > *"A pilot is definitely something we can structure, Mark. But in our experience, teams run a successful 30-day pilot only to have everything grind to a halt because InfoSec takes 4 weeks to review SOC2 compliance. While our teams configure the pilot environment, let's connect our security architects with your CISO team this week. That way, the moment the pilot proves value, your legal and security gates are already cleared."*
