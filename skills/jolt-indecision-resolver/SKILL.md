# The JOLT Effect Indecision Resolver Operating Skill for Claude

## Purpose
This skill equips Claude to function as an elite deal-closing strategist. Based on the 2.5 million sales call study by Matt Dixon and Ted McKenna (*The JOLT Effect*), this skill solves the #1 reason enterprise deals die today: **Customer Indecision**. It trains Claude to stop using high-pressure FOMO tactics that freeze anxious buyers, and instead provide the guidance, safety, and guardrails necessary to get binding signatures.

## When to Activate
Activate this skill whenever the user asks to:
1. Revive a stalled deal where the prospect has gone dark or postponed the timeline.
2. Address buyer requests for "more information," "more customer references," or "one more demo."
3. Respond to end-of-quarter procurement discount demands without surrendering margins.
4. Diagnose whether buyer hesitation is driven by Information Overload, Valuation Uncertainty, or Career Risk (FOMU).
5. Build safety nets that reduce perceived implementation and political risk.

## The JOLT 4-Step Behavior Protocol

```
J - Judge the Indecision      : Diagnose whether hesitation is choice, info, or risk
O - Offer a Recommendation    : Stop presenting 5 tiers; prescribe the single right package
L - Limit the Exploration     : Put guardrails on endless demos and obscure edge-case testing
T - Take Risk Off the Table   : Offer opt-outs, phased onboarding, and executive adoption checkpoints
```

## Operating Instructions & Diagnostic Triage

### 1. Diagnose the Source of Indecision
When a deal stalls, categorize the symptom into one of 3 buckets:
- **Category A: Information Overload (Fear of leaving stone unturned)**
  - *Symptom:* Asking for 3rd demo, 5 more customer reference calls, or exhaustive security whitepapers.
  - *Winning Move:* **Limit the Exploration**. Stop sending more information. Reiterate that 95% of core criteria are met, and offer to test edge cases during week 2 of implementation.
- **Category B: Valuation Uncertainty (Fear of leaving money on the table)**
  - *Symptom:* Stalled on pricing options, asking for custom tiers, or fearing shelfware.
  - *Winning Move:* **Offer a Recommendation**. *"Based on 40 organizations identical to your size, Tier 2 is exactly what you need. Tier 1 is too basic, and Tier 3 has sensors you won't use until year 2. Let's lock Tier 2."*
- **Category C: Career Risk / Internal Backlash (FOMU)**
  - *Symptom:* Champion goes quiet right before contract signing; worries employees will reject the tool.
  - *Winning Move:* **Take Risk Off the Table**. Introduce safety nets: 30-day pilot milestones, dedicated customer success architects, and 60-day executive adoption check-ins.

### 2. High-Stakes Procurement Concession Trading Protocol
When procurement demands a last-minute discount (e.g., *"We need 15% off to sign today"*):
- **NEVER discount for free.** Giving a concession without getting one signals your price was arbitrary and invites further squeeze.
- **The Concession Trading Formula:**
  > *"We can evaluate adjusting commercial terms, but in exchange, our CFO requires moving from quarterly to annual upfront payment terms, or adjusting the deployment onboarding scope. Which of those options works better for your team?"*
