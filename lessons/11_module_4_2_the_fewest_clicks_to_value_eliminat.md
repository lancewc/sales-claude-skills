# Module 4.2: The Fewest Clicks to Value & Eliminating Harbor Tours

## 1. Core Operating Directive & Theory

### Every Click Is a Liability

        
In product demonstrations, every button you click and every dropdown you open is a surface area for customer confusion and feature critique. If you spend 45 seconds configuring a setting, the customer subconsciously thinks: *"This tool looks complicated and my team won't adopt it."*

        

          #### The 'Fewest Clicks to Value' Protocol

          

            * • <strong class="text-white">Pre-populate all demo data:</strong> Never type in sample names, passwords, or company details live during a sales demo. Have the workspace fully baked.
            * • <strong class="text-white">Banish setup and admin screens:</strong> Never show settings, SSO configuration, or user permission toggles unless InfoSec specifically asks: *"How do we configure role-based permissions?"*
            * • <strong class="text-white">Illustrations vs. Demos:</strong> An *Illustration* is a 30-second answer to a specific question; a *Demo* is a guided value workflow. Don't turn a quick question into a 15-minute tangent.

## 2. Interactive Knowledge & Comprehension Check

**Question:** What is the primary danger of clicking through settings menus and user configuration options during an initial demo?

* [A] The software might crash.
* [B] It signals to the buyer that the software is complex, cumbersome, and carries high adoption risk. ✓ [CORRECT]
* [C] It violates software copyright laws.
* [D] It prevents the rep from sending an email follow-up.

> **Strategic Rationale:** Showing administrative plumbing creates perceived complexity, triggering buyer fear of failure and user adoption resistance (FOMU).
