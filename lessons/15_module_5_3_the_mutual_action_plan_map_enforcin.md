## 1. Core Operating Directive & Theory

### Why 'Hoping' to Close Is Not a Strategy

        
Rookie AEs send proposals and pray the customer signs by the end of the month. Professional enterprise AEs co-author a **Mutual Action Plan (MAP)** that establishes a shared timeline working backward from a critical business catalyst.

        

          #### The Reverse Timeline Engineering Protocol

          

            * **Anchor to the Business Catalyst:** *"Dave, your Chicago office lease expires Dec 15th, meaning you must finalize space reconfiguration by Nov 15th to avoid lease renewal penalties."*
            <li>**Work Backward:**
              

                • Target Go-Live: Nov 15

                • Employee Training & Space Setup: Nov 01 (takes 2 weeks)

                • Contract Execution: Oct 15 (gives IT 2 weeks to provision SSO)

                • Legal Redlines & Procurement Review: Oct 01 (takes 2 weeks)

                • CFO Business Case Presentation: Sep 20
              

            </li>
            * **Hold the Champion Accountable:** When a prospect asks to reschedule a call, you point to the MAP: *"We can move this to next week, but if we miss the Oct 1st legal redline milestone, we risk missing your Nov 15th lease deadline."*

## 2. Interactive Knowledge & Comprehension Check

**Question:** What is the primary anchor that gives a Mutual Action Plan (MAP) actual enforcement power?

* [A] The sales rep's quarterly quota deadline.
* [B] A compelling customer business catalyst or deadline (e.g., lease renewal, RTO policy date, fiscal year-end). ✓ [CORRECT]
* [C] A 10% discount expiring at midnight.
* [D] A threat to cancel the customer's account.

> **Strategic Rationale:** A MAP only has power when tied to the prospect's critical business deadline, making deal slippage an existential risk to their company.
